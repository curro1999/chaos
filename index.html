PhD encounters

In the course of my research I keep running into equations and models that I cannot leave alone. Some I need for my own work on fermentation. Others I simply stumbled on. All of them have turned out to be useful twice: to understand the model itself, and to see the moment in the history of science it came from.

This page is where I keep them. Each one is a small simulation you can play with, next to a short note on who wrote the equation and why. The aim is a space that is playful and informative. More will follow as the thesis goes on.

Chaos and nonlinear dynamics

  • The logistic map

    Open → x_next = r · x · (1 − x)

    Robert May's population rule. Turn one number up and a steady population starts to oscillate, then loses all pattern.

  • Period doubling and chaos

    Open → 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → … → chaos

    The bifurcation diagram of the same rule: every long-run behaviour for every value of r, in one picture you can zoom into.

  • The Lorenz attractor

    Open → dx/dt = σ(y − x), dy/dt = x(ρ − z) − y, dz/dt = xy − βz

    Two trajectories that start a millionth apart and end up unrelated, while both keep drawing the same butterfly.

  • The Mandelbrot set

    Open → z_next = z² + c

    The logistic map taken to the complex plane. One picture of every value of c, with the whole period-doubling story lying along its horizontal axis.

  • The Koch curve

    Open → D = log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.26

    Von Koch's 1904 curve with no tangent anywhere, and what it means for a shape to have a dimension between 1 and 2.

Populations and microbial growth

  • Predators and prey

    Open → dx/dt = αx − βxy, dy/dt = δxy − γy

    The Lotka–Volterra model. Two species that chase each other in cycles, and Volterra's answer to why less fishing favoured the sharks.

  • The Monod equation

    Open → μ = μmax · S / (Ks + S)

    How fast microbes grow as their food runs out, and the batch culture curve that follows from it.

Acidity and enzymes

  • The pH scale

    Open → pH = −log [H⁺]

    Sørensen's shorthand from the Carlsberg Laboratory. Dissolve an acid and see why ten times more of it moves the pH by one unit, or by less.

  • Michaelis–Menten kinetics

    Open → v = Vmax · [S] / (Km + [S])

    How fast an enzyme works as its substrate runs out, and what two kinds of inhibitor do to the curve.
