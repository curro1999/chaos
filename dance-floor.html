The dance floor

In 1909 the German biologist Richard Woltereck was raising water fleas (Daphnia) in tanks with different amounts of food, and noticed that the same lineage grew a different head shape in each tank. The organism had not changed; the water had. He called the full set of shapes a lineage can take, one for each environment, its Reaktionsnorm, the norm of reaction. Two years later Wilhelm Johannsen gave the two sides of that idea their names: the genotype is what is written in the cell, the phenotype is what the cell actually does.

phenotype = genotype × environmentthe same genotype, placed in two environments, gives two phenotypes

A fermentation is this sentence run with microbes. A yeast strain carries one genotype; the temperature, the sugar, the oxygen and the acidity of the tank decide which of its possible behaviours shows up, and those behaviours are the aroma, the alcohol and the acidity we taste. Below, the same idea in a form that needs no microscope: a club, a control booth behind the glass, and two knobs. Watch who dances, who goes to the bar and who leaves.

What the values mean

Temperature, music
The environment. The two things the booth controls. Nobody on the floor can change them.
Ravers, talkers, swayers
Three genotypes. Each person is born with a preferred music level and a heat tolerance and keeps them all night. Ravers want it loud and shrug off heat; talkers want it quiet and suffer when it is hot; swayers are in between and mostly copy the crowd. Think of them as three strains in the same tank.
Dancing, at the bar, leaving
The phenotype: what each person actually does. A raver on a quiet night and a raver on a loud night are the same genotype showing two behaviours.
Beers bought
A measurable product of the crowd's metabolism. Thirst builds with temperature and with how hard people dance; when it is high enough, a person walks to the bar, buys a beer and comes back.
Sweat index
Average of temperature times dancing effort over the people on the floor. A loud, hot night is a wet one. Like beer, it is something the booth never set but can read off the room.
Left the club
People whose comfort stayed low for too long. New people arrive at the door at a rate that follows the mood inside, so a good night fills up and a bad one empties.

Things to try

  1. Press Cold · quiet and wait half a minute. The talkers (orange) stay and chat, the ravers (blue) drift to the door. Same room, one genotype thrives, another leaves.
  2. Now press Cold · loud. Watch the colours swap roles: the ravers fill the floor and the talkers go. The genotypes did not change. The phenotypes did.
  3. Go Hot · loud. Beers per minute and the sweat index climb together, and even the ravers start walking to the bar. The product you can measure (beer, sweat) is not something you set; it comes out of what the crowd is doing to cope with the room.
  4. Hold the music at 50 % and slide the temperature slowly from 14 to 38 °C. The orange population falls off before the blue one. That curve, how one genotype's behaviour changes along one environmental axis, is Woltereck's norm of reaction.
  5. Untick Show genotypes and set any corner. With all dots the same grey you only see the phenotype, which is all a glass of fermented tea ever shows you. The genotype is still there, deciding who does what.
  6. Finally, replace the words. Temperature and music are temperature and dissolved oxygen. Ravers and talkers are Saccharomyces and a lactic acid bacterium. Beer is ethanol, sweat is acidity. The booth is the brewer.
